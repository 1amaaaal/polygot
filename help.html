How to play

Name the programming language in six guesses. A new snippet arrives every day at midnight, your local time.

Clues

You start with three lines of code. Every wrong guess shows two more lines and compares your guess with the answer:

Java Object-oriented 1995 ↑ Static VM C-like
  • Same as the answer
  • Year within five years of the answer
  • Different from the answer
Paradigm
Its main style: object-oriented, functional, procedural, multi-paradigm, logic or declarative.
Year
When it first appeared. The arrow points towards the answer: ↑ means the answer is newer.
Typing
Static (checked before running), dynamic (checked while running) or untyped.
Runs as
How it usually runs: native code, a virtual machine, an interpreter, a JIT compiler or transpiled to another language.
Looks like
The syntax family it resembles, such as C-like, Lisp or ML-like.

Hard mode

Switch it on in Settings before your first guess of the day. Syntax colouring is turned off, and every guess has to be a language that could still be the answer given the clues so far. Hard-mode scores get a star, like 3/6*.

Arcade

The arcade has the modes that don't affect your daily streak:

  • Endless: random snippets, one after another, until you miss one.
  • Speed run: as many as you can name in 60 seconds. Wrong guesses cost three seconds.
  • Practice: a single random snippet.
  • Archive: every past daily puzzle.

Leaderboard

There are three boards, each covering the last seven days. Daily wins earn points, from 6 for one guess down to 1 for six; the speed run and endless boards rank your best run of the week. To appear on them, sign in with GitHub on your profile. While there are only a few players, the boards are topped up with bots that play every day, labelled as bots.

Challenges

After a round, press Challenge a friend to copy a link to the same snippet with your score attached. Sign in on your profile so they know who sent it.

Badges and Wrapped

Your profile keeps your stats, the languages you've collected and 18 badges to unlock. Wrapped turns it all into a short summary you can share.

Installing it

Polyglot works offline and can be installed like an app. On Android, choose Add to Home screen from the browser menu; on an iPhone, Share → Add to Home Screen. On a computer, look for the install icon in the address bar.

Privacy

You can play without signing in, and then everything stays in your browser. If you sign in with GitHub, Polyglot stores your public GitHub username and avatar, your results and your saved progress, so it can show you on the leaderboard and sync between devices. It never sees your email or password, and there's no tracking or advertising.
